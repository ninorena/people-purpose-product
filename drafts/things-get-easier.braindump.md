I have a big announcement: my amazing wife Sophie and I welcomed our son Noah to the world!

Everyone talks about the things that get harder as parents (no sleep, diapers and spit up, etc.), but I want to share a bit more about how things have gotten so much easier.

People Purpose Product started as the focal point of the three things I have spent a lot of my career working on. I was introduced to the product field by way of the school of thought that said the biggest risk any product has is that it was built to solve the wrong thing for the wrong person. Sure, products fail because they are built in the wrong way, but the ultimate goal of a product delivering value to users and customers always stuck with me as to where I wanted to work as a product strategist and coach.

Fast forward to becoming a parent. It's cliche, but there's a reason cliches are true…I no longer question why I am doing what I am doing. It's so clear that the world has moved on from being my world to being my child's world. Everything I do is for him. When you have a clear purpose, there's a lot that actually becomes easier. It's crazy to think back to just a few months ago before we had Noah and look at what felt hard. I don't mean to take away from true hardship, and will always subscribe to difficulty being a relative force. Training to be an EMT in a former life, my instructor shared a wonderful tip: no matter who it is you are treating, if an ambulance has to show up to the scene, it's probably the worst day that person has experienced in their life. Could be a five year old who sprained their ankle playing soccer, or it could be a professional skydiver who broke both of her femurs on landing. Worst day of their life. So with that relative lens in mind, I can confidently say that doing things that take care of me so I can take care of Noah have gotten so much easier.

A note on prioritization: I've always thought of that exercise as one of saying no rather than saying yes, and it's helped me a ton. That said, figuring out what to say no to is MUCH harder than figuring out what to say yes to. And once again, this is something that has gotten so much easier for me since meeting the little fella. I know where I should be spending my time and energy with the utmost clarity.
